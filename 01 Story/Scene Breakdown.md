# Scene Breakdown

## Purpose

This document adapts the original short story into a visual sequence for the
animated film.

The original story remains the narrative source of truth:

[Original Short Story](Original%20Short%20Story.md)

This is not yet a shot list. It defines what happens in each scene, what the
audience needs to understand, Madrigal's emotional progression, and where
internal prose must be translated into visual storytelling.

Individual scenes will later be broken into production shots.

---

# Overall Story Structure

The film follows Madrigal through what initially appears to be an ordinary,
frustrating day of insignificant guardian-angel work.

She repeatedly prevents small accidents. Nobody sees her. Nobody thanks her.
Often nobody even realizes that anything happened.

After her shift ends, Madrigal encounters a young boy playing alone in the
snow. Unlike everyone else she has encountered, the boy can see her.

Their brief interaction gives Madrigal something she has been missing all day:
recognition and uncomplicated human connection.

When the boy's mother calls him home, Madrigal recognizes her as the woman she
helped earlier in the day.

Only then does Madrigal understand that the tiny, invisible interventions she
dismissed as meaningless were not meaningless at all.

The film ends with Madrigal seeing her work differently than she did at the
beginning.

# Sequence 1 — Another Day

## Scene 1 — Madrigal Arrives

**Location:** Exterior / town  
**Time:** Morning

### Purpose

Introduce Madrigal, establish the world, and immediately undermine any
expectation that being a guardian angel is glamorous.

### On Screen

Morning in the town.

Madrigal descends into the ordinary human world to begin another shift.

Her arrival should be visually beautiful, but Madrigal herself treats it as
routine. She has done this many times before.

She adjusts her clothing or satchel and prepares herself for work with the
energy of someone reporting for another long day.

### Madrigal

Tired.

Competent.

Not miserable, but clearly less enchanted with the job than someone called a
"guardian angel" might be expected to be.

### Audience Learns

- Madrigal is an angel.
- She works among humans.
- This is a job.
- She is already tired of some part of it.

### Tone

Quiet humor is appropriate.

The contrast between the magical appearance of an angel descending from the
sky and Madrigal's mundane attitude toward starting work can establish the
film's personality immediately.

### Transition

Madrigal heads toward her first assignment.

---

# Sequence 2 — The Restaurant

## Scene 2 — Breakfast Chaos

**Location:** Restaurant kitchen  
**Time:** Morning

### Purpose

Show what Madrigal actually does.

This should be the audience's first complete example of a guardian intervention.

### On Screen

A busy breakfast kitchen is already in motion.

Orders are being called.

Pans hiss.

Plates move through the pass.

People squeeze around one another.

Madrigal moves through the chaos largely unnoticed.

A small chain of events begins that will lead to an accident involving the
chef.

Madrigal sees the danger before anyone else does.

She intervenes.

The accident never happens.

The chef continues working.

### Important Principle

The intervention should be small.

Madrigal does not perform a spectacular miracle.

She changes one tiny thing.

That tiny thing changes what happens next.

### Madrigal

Initially alert and professional.

After preventing the accident, she waits for some reaction.

None comes.

Perhaps the chef pauses for half a second, sensing that something nearly
happened.

Madrigal watches hopefully.

Then the chef simply continues working.

### Audience Learns

This is what Madrigal's work looks like.

She prevents bad things that therefore appear never to have happened.

That creates the fundamental problem of her job:

**success is invisible.**

### Emotional Beat

A tiny disappointment.

Not devastation.

This has happened before.

# Sequence 3 — The Mother

## Scene 3 — The Car

**Location:** Car / road  
**Time:** Later that day

### Purpose

Plant the story element that will eventually reveal the significance of
Madrigal's work.

This scene must be memorable enough that the audience can recognize the mother
later without making the connection obvious too early.

### On Screen

A mother drives through town.

The drive is ordinary.

There may be signs of the distracted complexity of everyday life: things in
the passenger seat, children's belongings, traffic, errands, perhaps a phone
notification she ignores.

A child's sippy cup or similar object shifts.

A small chain of events begins.

Madrigal notices the danger.

Again, she makes a tiny intervention.

The potential accident never occurs.

The mother continues driving, unaware of Madrigal.

### Madrigal

She waits again.

Maybe this time the woman actually says something such as:

"That was close."

For a fraction of a second Madrigal looks hopeful.

But the woman is speaking to herself.

She cannot see Madrigal.

Madrigal's hope disappears.

### Audience Learns

The restaurant was not an isolated incident.

This is Madrigal's entire job.

### Critical Story Requirement

The mother's appearance must be distinctive enough that both Madrigal and the
audience can recognize her later.

Do not overemphasize her.

The audience should remember her subconsciously rather than immediately
thinking:

"This character must be important."

# Sequence 4 — The Work Nobody Sees

## Scene 4 — Guardian Montage

**Location:** Multiple locations  
**Time:** Day into late afternoon

### Purpose

Compress Madrigal's working day while reinforcing the theme.

### Structure

A series of short interventions.

Each begins with something mundane.

Each could become something worse.

Each is quietly redirected by Madrigal.

Possible examples can be developed during shot planning.

The important pattern is:

    ordinary situation
        ↓
    Madrigal notices danger
        ↓
    tiny intervention
        ↓
    nothing bad happens
        ↓
    human continues life
        ↓
    Madrigal receives no recognition

### Escalation

The montage should not merely repeat the same joke.

Madrigal's reaction changes.

Early:

She waits for acknowledgment.

Later:

She waits less.

Later still:

She prevents something and walks away before the person even reacts.

### Visual Storytelling

This is where the film can communicate what prose describes internally.

We do not need Madrigal to tell us:

"Nobody appreciates what I do."

We watch her stop expecting anyone to notice.

### Emotional Progression

Professional  
→ mildly disappointed  
→ weary  
→ resigned.

### End of Montage

The day is ending.

Madrigal's shift is nearly over.

---

# Sequence 5 — Snow

## Scene 5 — End of Shift

**Location:** Copper Creek exterior  
**Time:** Evening

### Purpose

Change the rhythm of the film.

The busy working day ends.

The emotional heart of the story begins.

### On Screen

Snow has begun falling heavily.

Copper Creek becomes quieter.

Warm windows glow against the cold blue evening.

Madrigal's workday is over.

For the first time all day, nobody needs anything from her.

She is alone.

### Sound

The soundtrack should noticeably open up here.

The earlier scenes may contain busy kitchens, traffic, voices, movement and
workplace noise.

Now:

- wind
- footsteps
- muffled distant interiors
- snowfall atmosphere
- space

### Madrigal

Exhausted.

A little lonely.

She begins heading home.

### Visual Importance

This scene establishes the visual language that will dominate the remainder of
the film:

- deep blue winter exterior
- warm golden windows
- falling snow
- Madrigal isolated between pools of human warmth

---

# Sequence 6 — The Boy in the Snow

## Scene 6 — Madrigal Notices the Boy

**Location:** Snow-covered street / yard / open area  
**Time:** Evening

### Purpose

Introduce the boy and create the story's emotional turn.

### On Screen

Madrigal notices a young boy outside in the snow.

He is playing.

At first she may instinctively observe him as though he were another potential
assignment.

Is he in danger?

Does she need to intervene?

No.

He is simply a child enjoying the snow.

Madrigal watches.

Then something unexpected happens.

The boy looks directly at her.

### Madrigal

She assumes at first that he is looking through her or past her.

She moves slightly.

His eyes follow.

She looks behind herself.

Nobody is there.

The boy is looking at **her**.

### Emotional Beat

For perhaps the first time in the film, Madrigal is genuinely surprised.

### Audience Learns

The boy can see her.

Do not explain why.

The fact itself is enough.

---

# Sequence 7 — Play

## Scene 7 — Madrigal and the Boy

**Location:** Same  
**Time:** Evening

### Purpose

Give Madrigal the emotional experience she has been denied throughout the
film.

Someone sees her.

Not because she performed a miracle.

Not because she saved him.

He simply sees her.

### On Screen

The boy invites Madrigal into whatever he is doing.

Madrigal initially hesitates.

She is technically off duty.

She may not quite know how to interact with a child who can actually see her.

Then she joins him.

They play in the snow.

### Possible Activities

Exact actions should be determined during shot planning, but possibilities
include:

- throwing snow
- making something in the snow
- sliding
- chasing
- laughing
- the boy becoming fascinated by her wings
- a playful interaction involving feathers or snow

Avoid turning the sequence into a magical spectacle.

Its power comes from how ordinary it is.

### Madrigal

Her physicality changes.

Earlier she moved like someone at work.

Now she loosens.

She laughs.

She forgets about her shift.

For a little while she is simply present.

### Emotional Purpose

This is the first time today that Madrigal receives something back from the
world.

---

# Sequence 8 — Called Home

## Scene 8 — The Mother's Voice

**Location:** Same / nearby home  
**Time:** Evening

### Purpose

Begin the story's reveal.

### On Screen

A voice calls for the boy.

He reacts.

His mother is calling him home.

The boy doesn't want the moment to end, but he has to go.

He says goodbye to Madrigal.

He runs toward home.

### Madrigal

She watches him leave.

There is a small sadness, but it is different from the sadness earlier in the
film.

This moment mattered.

Then Madrigal sees the mother.

She recognizes her.

### Recognition

This should not require dialogue.

A look is enough.

If useful, sound or imagery from the earlier driving scene may briefly echo
here.

Do not over-explain the connection.

Trust the audience.

---

# Sequence 9 — Understanding

## Scene 9 — The Window

**Location:** Exterior of the family's home  
**Time:** Night

### Purpose

Deliver the thematic payoff.

### On Screen

The boy is now safely inside.

Madrigal remains outside.

Through a warm window, she sees the boy with his mother.

The domestic moment should be completely ordinary.

That is precisely why it matters.

Perhaps the mother:

- helps remove his wet coat
- dries his hair
- gives him something warm
- laughs at something he says
- embraces him
- returns to an ordinary evening routine

Nothing miraculous happens.

### Madrigal's Realization

Earlier that day, Madrigal prevented a tiny accident involving this woman.

At the time, it felt meaningless.

Now the woman is here.

The boy is here.

This evening exists because countless ordinary moments were allowed to
continue.

Madrigal does not need to know exactly what would have happened without her
intervention.

Neither does the audience.

The point is that small acts matter precisely because their consequences are
larger than the moment in which they occur.

### Performance

Keep Madrigal's reaction restrained.

No crying breakdown.

No speech.

Perhaps:

- recognition
- stillness
- breath
- eyes soften
- the smallest smile

This is the emotional culmination of the film.

---

# Sequence 10 — Home

## Scene 10 — Madrigal Leaves

**Location:** Exterior / Copper Creek / sky  
**Time:** Night

### Purpose

Resolve Madrigal's emotional arc and end on an image rather than an
explanation.

### On Screen

Madrigal watches the warm window for another moment.

Then she turns away.

She walks into the falling snow.

Her posture is subtly different from earlier.

Nothing about her job has changed.

Nobody suddenly knows what she does.

Nobody thanks her.

Tomorrow will presumably contain more kitchens, cars, spilled drinks, near
misses and people who never know she was there.

But Madrigal understands the work differently now.

### Flight

Madrigal takes flight.

Production does not necessarily need to show one continuous ground-to-air
takeoff.

A cinematic transition may work better:

    Madrigal looks upward
        ↓
    wings unfold
        ↓
    strong wingbeat / snow disturbance
        ↓
    CUT
        ↓
    Madrigal already rising above Copper Creek

She flies over the town.

Below her are hundreds of illuminated windows.

Each represents an ordinary life continuing.

### Final Image

Madrigal disappears into the snowy night.

Copper Creek remains below:

small,

warm,

alive.

Fade to black.

# Madrigal's Emotional Arc

The film should make Madrigal's emotional progression legible without requiring
explanatory dialogue.

| Stage | Madrigal |
| --- | --- |
| Beginning | Tired but professional |
| Restaurant | Hopeful for acknowledgment |
| Car | Disappointed |
| Montage | Increasingly resigned |
| End of shift | Lonely / emotionally depleted |
| Boy sees her | Surprised |
| Playing | Open / joyful |
| Boy leaves | Bittersweet |
| Mother revealed | Recognition |
| Window | Understanding |
| Ending | Quietly renewed |

The ending should not transform Madrigal into an exuberant believer in her
calling.

The change is smaller and more credible.

She simply understands that unseen work is not the same thing as meaningless
work.

---

# Dialogue Philosophy

Keep dialogue minimal.

The film's strongest ideas should be communicated through:

- action
- reaction
- visual cause and effect
- repeated motifs
- sound
- editing
- Madrigal's performance

Avoid dialogue whose primary purpose is explaining the theme.

In particular, Madrigal should not explicitly explain what she learns at the
end.

The audience should arrive at the realization with her.

---

# Recurring Visual Motifs

## Windows

Windows represent lives Madrigal can observe but rarely participate in.

Early in the film, windows may emphasize separation.

At the end, the warm family window becomes evidence of the life her invisible
work helps preserve.

## Hands

Human hands perform ordinary actions throughout the film:

- cooking
- driving
- holding cups
- helping children
- preparing food
- opening doors

Because generative video currently struggles with hands, these should be
planned carefully rather than avoided entirely.

## Wings

Madrigal's wings should reflect her emotional state subtly.

They are functional anatomy, not an emotional semaphore, but posture can
contribute to performance:

- folded/tucked while working
- slightly drooped when exhausted
- freer during play
- opening confidently at the end

## Warmth and Cold

The film repeatedly contrasts:

**cold blue exterior space**

with

**warm golden human spaces.**

Madrigal often occupies the boundary between them.

# Sound Progression

Sound can reinforce Madrigal's emotional journey.

## Working Day

Dense and practical:

- kitchen activity
- traffic
- voices
- machinery
- footsteps
- everyday noise

## Evening

The soundscape becomes sparse:

- winter wind
- snow-muted town ambience
- distant interiors
- Madrigal's footsteps

## The Boy

Human sound returns:

- laughter
- movement in snow
- breathing
- playful activity

## Window

The warm interior is audible but muffled.

Madrigal remains outside.

## Flight

The town recedes.

Wind and wings become dominant.

Music may gently open here without becoming triumphant.

# Adaptation Principles

## Preserve the Original Story

The short story is the narrative source.

Changes made for film should solve cinematic problems rather than casually
rewrite the story.

## Show Rather Than Explain

Internal prose should become:

- behavior
- expression
- visual repetition
- cause and effect
- sound
- editing

## Keep Miracles Small

Madrigal's interventions should be almost mundane.

The story becomes more powerful when the audience understands that tiny actions
can have enormous consequences.

## Protect the Reveal

The mother must be recognizable when she returns, but her earlier scene should
not announce that she is important.

## Trust the Audience

Do not explain every causal relationship.

The audience should be allowed to recognize the mother and understand the
meaning of the moment themselves.

## Keep the Ending Restrained

The emotional payoff should come from Madrigal's realization, not spectacle.

The final flight is release, not victory.

# Production Steps

With the scene structure established, production planning can proceed one scene
at a time.

For each scene:

1. Create a shot list.
2. Identify required canonical character/location/prop references.
3. Design each shot for a controlled AI-generated action.
4. Generate and approve the 16:9 starting frame.
5. Write the video-generation prompt.
6. Generate candidate video clips.
7. Select the best take.
8. Assemble the scene in DaVinci Resolve.
9. Add temporary sound design and music.
10. Review the completed scene before proceeding.
