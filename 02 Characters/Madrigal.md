# Madrigal

## Role

Madrigal is the protagonist, an entry-level guardian angel whose small, unseen interventions embody the film's theme of unnoticed kindness. She should feel like a person with a job to do, not someone dressed in an angel costume.

## Canonical Visual Design

Madrigal has a young-adult appearance. She is petite and slender, but never childlike, with relatively long, natural body proportions and a balanced head-to-body ratio. Her face has expressive eyes and freckles. Her dark hair is usually gathered into a loose, slightly messy bun.

Her winter clothing is practical and lived-in:

- A dark winter coat
- A blue-gray scarf
- Worn boots
- A leather satchel
- Feathered wings that feel like functional anatomy

The wings should have believable size, structure, attachment, feather overlap, and weight. The design should suggest that Madrigal works in winter conditions rather than presenting her as a ceremonial or decorative angel.

## Visual Style

Render Madrigal with a painterly, storybook animated-film quality. Favor expressive shapes, handcrafted texture, and soft edges where appropriate. Do not drift toward photorealistic CGI.

## Continuity Requirements

All new visual assets should derive from the canonical character reference rather than independently reinterpreting Madrigal for each shot. Preserve:

- Apparent age
- Facial identity and proportions
- Body height and proportions
- Head-to-body ratio
- Hair color, style, and silhouette
- Coat design and length
- Blue-gray scarf
- Boots
- Leather satchel
- Wing size
- Wing shape and feather structure
- Wing attachment points
- Overall silhouette
- Painterly rendering style

POC testing showed that source-image generation can produce more visible character drift than the video animation model. A video generation may preserve a flawed source frame faithfully, so each source image must be checked against the canonical design before animation.

## Performance Guidance

Madrigal's acting should generally remain restrained and human. Build her performance through:

- Eye movement
- Breathing
- Posture
- Hesitation
- Sighs
- Small smiles
- Subtle physical reactions

Avoid broad cartoon acting and exaggerated gestures unless a specific story moment intentionally requires them.
