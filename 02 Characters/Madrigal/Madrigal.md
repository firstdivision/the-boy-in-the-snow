# Madrigal

## Role

Madrigal is the protagonist, an entry-level guardian angel whose small, unseen interventions embody the film's theme of unnoticed kindness. She should feel like a person with a job to do, not someone dressed in an angel costume.

The user accepted [madrigal-character-sheet-take02.png](madrigal-character-sheet-take02.png) as Madrigal's canonical character sheet on 2026-11-23 with "let's use this one". Use this exact file for her face, adult proportions, hair, wardrobe, accessories, near-ground folded wings, and illustrated rendering. It supersedes the [original sheet](madrigal-character-sheet.png), preserved as a development reference, and take01, whose local image was subsequently removed at the user's request. ## Canonical Visual Design

Madrigal has a young-adult appearance. She is petite and slender, but never childlike, with relatively long, natural body proportions and a balanced head-to-body ratio. Her face has expressive brown eyes and freckles across the nose and cheeks. Preserve the existing sheet's facial proportions and complexion. Her dark-brown hair is usually gathered into a loose, slightly messy bun, with loose curls framing her face.

During development, the user requested the original sheet's younger, smoother face and larger expressive eyes. The correction only partly restored that earlier identity; after reviewing the result, the user accepted take02. Its face and bun are now the visual authority. Preserve that accepted appearance consistently rather than combining faces from different sheets.

Her winter clothing is practical and lived-in:

- A long dark blue-gray winter coat, reaching approximately mid-calf, with the existing sheet's buttons, pockets, cuffs, and hood construction
- A blue-gray scarf wrapped around her neck, with fringed ends hanging in front
- A cream dress with a lace-edged hem visible below the coat
- Dark stockings
- Worn brown leather lace-up boots
- A small round locket on a chain; do not invent an inscription or religious symbolism
- A worn brown leather cross-body satchel resting at her left hip, with the strap crossing from her right shoulder; preserve the flap and buckle construction shown in take02
- Two white-to-ivory feathered wings that feel like functional anatomy, attached consistently to her upper back; preserve the existing sheet's folded silhouette, layered feathers, and long trailing tips

The wings should have believable size, structure, attachment, feather overlap, and weight. The design should suggest that Madrigal works in winter conditions rather than presenting her as a ceremonial or decorative angel.

The user clarified on 2026-11-23 that her folded wings must almost reach the ground: the longest trailing feathers end just above the floor beside her boots. Preserve that length consistently in front, profile, and back views; the waist-length wings in the first new-sheet candidate are not approved.

## Visual Style

Render Madrigal with a painterly, storybook animated-film quality. Favor expressive shapes, handcrafted texture, and soft edges where appropriate. Do not drift toward photorealistic CGI.

The accepted take02 establishes her rendering alongside the other characters: controlled illustrated linework, soft painterly shading, textured fabrics, and expressive features. The [Mother sheet](<../The Mother/the-mother-character-sheet.png>) supplied style guidance during development; Madrigal's accepted take02 now governs her own identity, costume, and rendering.

## Performance Guidance

Madrigal's acting should generally remain restrained and human. Build her performance through:

- Eye movement
- Breathing
- Posture
- Hesitation
- Sighs
- Small smiles
- Subtle physical reactions

Avoid broad cartoon acting and exaggerated gestures unless a specific story moment intentionally requires them.

## Story and Wing Continuity

Her work is unseen by the people she helps, including The Chef and The Mother. The Boy can see her and invites her to play. Preserve her adult scale beside his small five-to-six-year-old design; The Chef is moderately taller than her without his hat.

The original story describes very large unfolded wings: each nearly twice her body length, with trailing feathers longer than The Boy is tall. The accepted sheet shows folded wings rather than an extended-wing scale study. Preserve its near-ground folded length and feather silhouette; do not infer a smaller flight span from the folded outline. An extended-wing reference would need a separate reviewed composition. The wings can wrap around her for comfort and curve around The Boy during their play; their attachment and anatomy remain coherent.
