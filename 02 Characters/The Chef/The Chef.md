# The Chef

## Role

The Chef is the experienced head chef at the Copper Kettle restaurant and the subject of Madrigal's first intervention. His unknowing reaction to her help begins her day's discouragement and illustrates how easily small acts of care go unrecognized.

The written design below was approved on 2026-11-23. A [candidate visual character sheet](the-chef-character-sheet.png) was generated that day, with [generation notes](character-sheet-image-prompt.md#generation-record) in its prompt document. It has not been accepted as canonical: the generated beard contains substantial gray despite the approved dark beard, and its rendering is flatter and more outlined than the painterly kitchen references. The written design remains the authority for those details.

## Story Facts

These events are established by the original short story:

- During a chaotic breakfast service, he instructs junior chefs in making a soufflé while chopping an onion.
- His attention is divided between teaching and his knife work. Madrigal redirects the blade before it can remove the tip of his finger.
- The knife slips from his hand. Unaware of the injury he narrowly avoided, he curses loudly.
- He cannot see Madrigal and never acknowledges her intervention.
- He picks up the knife and finishes chopping, muttering under his breath.
- He remains unharmed. His continued ability to feed people becomes part of Madrigal's later understanding of her work's value.

The scene notes establish him as competent and experienced: this is a moment of distraction during busy service.

## Canonical Visual Design

These are the approved written design choices. Preserve his existing Scene 02 appearance, using the kitchen frames linked below as the visual baseline.

- Middle-aged male appearance.
- Stocky build with a broad, grounded silhouette and natural adult proportions.
- Moderately taller than Madrigal when both stand upright, excluding his tall hat. His build supplies much of his physical presence.
- Preserve the facial proportions, heavy brows, complexion, full dark beard, and mustache visible in the existing kitchen frames. Do not turn the expression of a particular frame into a permanent scowl.
- Short dark brown hair, lightly graying at the temples beneath his hat. Keep the beard and mustache dark as established.

Use the same painterly, storybook animated-film language as Madrigal: expressive illustrated features, handcrafted texture, and soft edges where appropriate. Preserve the face's readability for restrained acting rather than adding photorealistic skin or materials.

## Clothing and Props

- Tall white chef's hat, preserving its established silhouette.
- White double-breasted chef's coat with the sleeves rolled to expose the forearms, consistent with the kitchen frames.
- Dark pinstriped bib apron, tied behind the waist. Preserve the stripe direction and apron construction across front, side, and back views.
- Dark charcoal straight-leg work trousers.
- Sturdy black leather kitchen shoes, visibly worn but maintained.

Keep the clothing practical and consistent with an active working kitchen. The back view must show the same coat, apron straps, waist ties, trousers, and footwear as the front and side views.

The chef's knife, onion, and cutting board belong to his scene activity. They are scene props rather than permanent accessories or required elements of a neutral character sheet.

## Personality and Performance

The approved temperament is demanding but invested in teaching his junior cooks. He is confident, authoritative, and accustomed to running his kitchen. His imposing presence should still feel human.

His movements are practiced and economical. During instruction he can speak emphatically and gesture briefly with his free hand; the performance remains controlled rather than broad comedy. The kitchen's noise explains his raised voice.

After the knife falls, his reaction progresses from surprise and bewilderment to irritation, followed by muttering as he resumes work. This outburst is a response to the immediate incident, not evidence of constant anger or cruelty. Keep his attention on his work and the people he can actually see.

## Relationships and Scale

The junior cooks respect his experience and attend to his instruction. His demanding manner is grounded in investment in their learning.

Madrigal stands close enough to intervene, but he remains unaware of her. They must not exchange meaningful looks, speech, or gestures. Her disappointment comes from his reaction to the fallen knife, not from any deliberate rejection by him.

When staging them together, maintain his moderately greater height without the hat and his broad build beside Madrigal's petite, slender adult proportions. Preserve her canonical design rather than changing her scale to accommodate him.

## Continuity Requirements

Preserve across shots and all views of the future character sheet:

- Apparent age, facial identity, heavy brows, complexion, and facial proportions.
- Stocky build, natural adult proportions, and height relative to Madrigal.
- Short dark brown hair, lightly graying temples, full dark beard, and mustache.
- Tall hat shape, white double-breasted coat, and rolled sleeves.
- Dark apron pinstripes, bib construction, straps, and ties behind the waist.
- Charcoal trousers and worn but maintained black leather kitchen shoes.
- Painterly rendering consistent with the film and Madrigal's approved sheet.

The existing kitchen frames establish the visible design baseline; the approved written choices resolve hair and lower-body details that those frames do not show reliably. A future sheet must depict one consistent design in a full-length front view, full-length side view, full-length back view, and face close-up. It becomes a canonical visual sheet only after review and acceptance.

For the knife incident, retain intact fingers and consistent hand, knife, onion, and board continuity. Do not introduce injury, exaggerated knife waving, or acknowledgment of Madrigal.
