# The Mother

## Role

The Mother is the woman Madrigal helps during the daytime driving incident and later recognizes welcoming The Boy home. Her return connects an apparently small intervention to an ordinary evening of safety and affection, helping Madrigal understand the importance of her unseen work.

## Story Facts

- She drives with children in the back seat. While turned partly around to hand one of them a sippy cup of juice, she allows the car to veer toward a ditch on the right.
- Madrigal nudges the steering wheel left, straightening the car. The jolt causes the mother to drop the cup in the back, the children cry, and she becomes angry, unaware of the accident that was prevented.
- Madrigal briefly flies alongside the car to ensure the danger has passed before leaving.
- That evening, she calls The Boy home from the steps of a house near the snowy park.
- He runs to her, speaks to her, and points toward Madrigal. She smiles, looks toward the park, nods to her son, and ushers him inside.
- She cannot see Madrigal. Madrigal recognizes her as the woman from the car earlier that day.
- Later, mother and son are visible in profile through a warmly lit curtained window, sitting at a table eating dinner. The story suggests soup only as Madrigal's imagining, not as a confirmed meal.

The story does not specify her exact age, physical features, or clothing. The scene breakdown requires her appearance to remain recognizable across the car and home scenes without announcing the later connection too strongly.

## Canonical Visual Design

These are the approved written design choices:

- She reads as a woman in her mid-to-late 30s, approximately 36–38, of subtly above-average height and average build, with natural adult proportions. Her face has slightly less youthful cheek fullness and delicate expression lines around the eyes and smile.
- She has fair skin, a softly oval face, dark eyebrows, plain brown eyes, and light freckles.
- Her irises should be neutral medium-dark brown, including their lighter areas. The user's latest instruction is plain brown and supersedes the earlier amber/gold interpretation and exact color matching to The Boy's sheet.
- Her dark-brown hair is worn in one long, loose braid beginning at the nape, lying along the center of her back, and reaching mid-back. It stays behind her shoulders and torso, hidden behind her body from the front, rather than draping over either shoulder.
- Her hair color, eyes, eyebrows, and freckles establish a family resemblance to The Boy while preserving her own adult facial identity.
- The braid provides a recognizable silhouette in the car, at the doorway, and in the final domestic scene. Preserve its length and placement down her back across views.
- Render her in the film's painterly, handcrafted storybook style, with illustrated texture, expressive features, and soft edges where appropriate.

## Clothing and Props

Her approved outdoor clothing is practical winter wear:

- A knee-length, muted forest-green wool coat with brown buttons and simple pockets.
- An oatmeal-colored scarf.
- Worn brown boots.

Underneath, she wears a soft cream cable-knit sweater with a round ribbed neckline, ribbed cuffs and hem, and practical charcoal trousers. The accepted indoor sheet shows these garments with the same worn brown boots and the coat and scarf removed. These layers form her indoor outfit for the final home scene. Keep the same underlying garments when moving between outdoor and indoor staging.

The sippy cup of juice is a scene prop from the driving incident, not a permanent character accessory.

## Personality and Performance

Her everyday energy is that of a busy parent managing several demands at once. The driving incident includes a brief, situational flash of frustration. Her reunion with her son shows quiet affection and familiarity.

Favor restrained, human performance through shifts in attention, a small tightening of the face, breathing, posture, and a softened expression or small smile toward her son. Her warmth at home should feel ordinary and lived-in, consistent with the film's focus on everyday care.

## Relationships and Scale

She is The Boy's mother. Maintain a clear adult-to-child scale difference beside his approved five-to-six-year-old, compact proportions.

She cannot see Madrigal and does not knowingly acknowledge her intervention. At the doorway, her response is to her son's account and pointing; looking toward the park does not indicate that she sees Madrigal.
