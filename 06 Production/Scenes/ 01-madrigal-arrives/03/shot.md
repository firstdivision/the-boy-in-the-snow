# Shot 01C — Landing

## Status

Starting Frame

## Estimated Duration

~4 seconds

## Purpose

Transition Madrigal naturally from her flight over Copper Creek to the ground
near her first assignment.

This is not a dramatic superhero landing. Madrigal lands here every day. The
movement should feel practiced, soft, and routine.

## Composition

Wide shot of a quiet snowy side street or alley near the restaurant.

Madrigal is the primary subject but enough of the surrounding environment is
visible to establish that she has arrived in the town seen beneath her in the
previous shots.

Warm light from the restaurant or nearby buildings contrasts with the cold
blue predawn exterior.

## Time / Lighting

Predawn / early winter morning.

Cool blue ambient light.

Warm golden light spills from nearby windows and the restaurant service
entrance.

Snow continues falling.

## Camera

Mostly static wide shot.

Very subtle cinematic push toward Madrigal is acceptable.

No camera orbit.

No handheld movement.

No dramatic camera move.

## Action

The shot begins with Madrigal already in the final few feet of her descent.

She is approximately two to three feet above the snowy ground.

Her wings are fully extended and supporting her descent.

Madrigal descends gently.

Her boots contact the snow.

Her knees bend naturally to absorb the small landing.

One final slow, powerful wingbeat displaces loose powdery snow around her feet.

Her coat, scarf, hair, satchel, and feathers respond naturally to the downward
airflow.

After landing, Madrigal steadies herself.

Her wings remain extended briefly as the motion settles.

She does not immediately walk away.

Hold for a small natural breath at the end of the shot.

## Character

Madrigal.

Maintain canonical appearance:

- young adult woman
- dark hair in a loose messy bun
- youthful facial features
- dark blue-gray winter coat
- thick scarf
- winter boots
- brown leather work satchel
- large natural white feathered wings
- normal adult proportions

Her appearance must match Shot 01B.

## Mood / Tone

Quiet.

Routine.

Grounded.

The landing should contrast with the beauty of her aerial flight.

Madrigal isn't making an entrance.

She has simply arrived at work.

## Starting Image Requirements

Madrigal begins approximately two to three feet above the snow.

Her wings are fully extended and positioned naturally to slow her descent.

Her legs are beneath her rather than trailing behind her.

Her knees are slightly bent in preparation for landing.

The restaurant/service area is visible nearby.

Warm interior light provides contrast against the cold predawn street.

## Video Model

Kling 3.0 Pro

## Video Prompt

Cinematic wide shot of the winged young woman in the reference image completing a gentle landing on a quiet snowy side street just before sunrise.

Preserve the appearance, clothing, hairstyle, wings, body proportions, architecture, lighting, colors, composition, and painterly animated-film aesthetic shown in the reference image.

The shot begins with the woman already only two to three feet above the snowy ground, completing the final moment of her descent.

She descends slowly and naturally. Her large feathered wings remain extended and perform one controlled final wingbeat to gently reduce her downward speed.

Her boots touch the soft snow. Her knees bend slightly and naturally as her weight settles onto the ground. Her movement is balanced, graceful, and relaxed.

The final wingbeat pushes air downward, stirring a small amount of loose powdery snow outward around her boots. The snow movement is subtle and physically believable.

Her coat hem, scarf, loose strands of hair, leather satchel, and outer wing feathers respond naturally to the airflow.

After her boots touch the ground, she steadies herself. Her wings remain extended briefly and then begin to relax as the motion settles. She takes a small natural breath.

She does not walk away during this shot.

Snow continues falling naturally throughout the entire environment at multiple depths. Some flakes pass close to the camera while finer snow falls throughout the distant street. Warm light from nearby windows contrasts with the cool blue morning.

The camera remains nearly stationary with only a very subtle cinematic push forward.

The landing is routine and effortless, not dramatic. Avoid a superhero-style landing, deep crouching, stumbling, jumping, running, or exaggerated impact.

Do not introduce additional characters or objects. Preserve the woman's appearance and proportions throughout the shot.

Quiet, restrained, painterly animated-film realism.

Approximately 4 seconds.

## Sound

- quiet winter ambience continuing from previous shots
- increasing airflow during final descent
- one substantial but soft wingbeat
- feather movement
- coat/scarf movement
- soft boots contacting snow
- subtle powder displacement
- small settling crunch
- Madrigal's quiet breath

Music continues underneath.

Avoid large fantasy whooshes or impact sounds.

## Transition

Cut from Shot 01B while Madrigal is still airborne.

Shot 01C begins already in the final stage of descent.

Cut to Shot 01D after her feet are planted, her body has settled, and the
landing motion is complete.

## Generation Notes

Watch specifically for:

- feet penetrating the ground
- legs becoming stiff
- unnatural knee movement
- superhero landing poses
- excessive snow explosion
- wings changing size
- extra wings appearing
- wings clipping through Madrigal
- satchel disappearing
- clothing changing
- Madrigal becoming shorter or younger
- camera unexpectedly following or orbiting her
- background snow becoming static

The most important thing is a believable transition of weight:

    airborne
        ↓
    boots touch snow
        ↓
    knees absorb weight
        ↓
    body settles

If Kling struggles with the complete motion, shorten the action further and
begin with Madrigal's boots immediately above the snow.

## Production Notes

This is the first production shot testing a controlled flight-to-ground
transition.

Do not ask the video model to transition from full forward flight into the
landing. The edit between Shots 01B and 01C deliberately removes that difficult
motion.

The shot succeeds if the audience accepts the landing without thinking about
it.
