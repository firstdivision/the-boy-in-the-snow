# Shot 03 — The Distracted Chef

## Status

Starting Frame Needed

## Estimated Duration

~5 seconds

## Purpose

Shift the audience's attention from Madrigal to the head chef and establish
the circumstances leading to the accident.

The chef is doing two things at once:

- instructing his junior chefs on the proper way to make a soufflé
- chopping an onion without giving the knife his full attention

He is competent and experienced, but distracted.

The danger should begin to register near the end of the shot without becoming
the primary visual focus yet.

## Story Beat

"The chef was busy instructing his junior chefs in the correct way to make
a soufflé, and he was not paying attention to where his knife was."

## Composition

Medium shot centered on the head chef at a wooden preparation station.

The chef stands behind the work surface with:

- an onion
- chef's knife
- cutting board
- soufflé ingredients / mixing bowl nearby

Two junior chefs stand near him, listening to his instruction.

The chef's upper body, hands, cutting board, knife, and onion should all be
readable within the frame.

The composition should allow the audience to understand both activities:

    teaching
       +
    chopping

Madrigal does not need to appear in this shot.

## Environment

Maintain the Copper Kettle kitchen established in Shots 01 and 02.

Visible background elements may include:

- warm amber practical lights
- aged cream tile
- copper cookware
- dark wood
- stove activity
- steam
- cooking haze
- moving kitchen staff

The background remains active but should be less visually dominant than in
Shot 01.

## Head Chef

Maintain the chef established in Shots 01 and 02:

- middle-aged
- stocky
- experienced
- expressive
- authoritative
- traditional white chef's uniform
- tall white chef's hat
- dark apron

He is confident and completely comfortable in his kitchen.

He is NOT incompetent.

His mistake happens because his attention is divided during a hectic
breakfast service.

## Junior Chefs

Two junior chefs listen while continuing simple preparation work.

They respect the chef and are paying attention to his explanation.

Their movements should remain restrained so the chef remains the focus.

## Action

The chef explains soufflé technique while standing at the preparation
counter.

He speaks emphatically to the junior chefs.

He gestures briefly with his free hand.

At the same time, he continues chopping an onion.

His eyes repeatedly move toward the junior chefs rather than remaining on
the cutting board.

The knife continues moving in a practiced chopping rhythm.

His chopping hand gradually moves farther across the onion.

Near the end of the shot, his attention remains on the junior chefs while
his knife hand continues working.

The audience should begin to feel that something is wrong.

Do NOT show an injury.

Do NOT complete the dangerous chop.

## Performance

The chef is animated and authoritative.

His body language says:

    Pay attention.
    This is how you do it properly.

There is a slight irony in the fact that he is lecturing other cooks about
proper technique while failing to pay attention to his own knife.

Do not play this as broad comedy.

The chef genuinely knows what he is doing.

He simply isn't looking.

## Camera

Medium cinematic shot.

Camera is positioned across the preparation counter at approximately
chest height.

Use an extremely slow push toward the chef and cutting board.

The camera movement subtly redirects attention from the larger kitchen toward
his hands.

No rapid zoom.

No dramatic camera movement.

No orbit.

His attention is divided.

controlled chopping motion on the cutting board.

movements.

Do not show an injury. Do not show blood. Do not show the knife contacting

Background kitchen activity continues naturally and subtly. Steam rises from

## Production Notes

The irony of this shot should remain understated.

The chef is teaching proper technique while making a tiny safety mistake of
his own.

The audience should not yet be thinking:

    He's going to cut his finger.

They should simply begin thinking:

    He really should be watching that knife.

Shot 04 turns that unease into immediate danger.
