# Shot 04 — The Danger

## Status

Starting Frame Needed

## Estimated Duration

~3 seconds

## Purpose

Reveal the danger Madrigal has arrived to prevent.

The previous shot established that the chef is distracted while chopping
an onion.

This shot shows exactly why that matters.

The chef's fingertip has moved into the path of the knife.

The audience should understand the danger immediately.

## Story Beat

"The knife was incorrectly positioned above the onion, ready to chop down -
an act that would have removed the tip of the chef's finger had Madrigal
not been there..."

## Composition

Tight close-up of the chef's hands, knife, onion, and cutting board.

The chef's face is NOT necessary.

The onion sits on the worn wooden cutting board.

One hand holds the chef's knife.

The other steadies the onion.

The fingertips of the hand holding the onion have gradually moved into an
unsafe position.

The knife is raised slightly above the onion.

One fingertip is visibly in the path of the blade.

The danger should be immediately understandable without being graphic.

## Visual Style

Maintain the established painterly animated-film aesthetic.

Even in close-up:

- hands remain stylized
- surfaces remain painterly
- warm amber kitchen lighting
- worn wooden cutting board
- soft cinematic depth of field
- realistic enough movement to communicate danger
- NOT photographic or live-action

## Action

The chef continues his established chopping rhythm.

The shot begins during the normal motion:

    CHOP

The knife rises again.

His other hand shifts slightly forward on the onion.

The knife begins another downward motion.

This time his fingertip is directly in its path.

CUT BEFORE CONTACT.

Nothing touches the finger in this shot.

There is no injury.

The entire shot exists to create one clear thought in the audience:

    That's going to hit his finger.

## Knife Movement

Keep the knife movement simple and controlled.

The knife:

1. begins near the cutting board
2. rises naturally for the next chop
3. pauses only briefly at the top of the motion
4. begins moving downward

CUT before it reaches the chef's hand.

Do not use exaggerated slow motion unless needed in editing.

The danger should come from positioning rather than dramatic knife movement.

## Camera

Locked close-up.

No pan.

No orbit.

No camera shake.

An extremely subtle cinematic push toward the knife and fingertip is
acceptable.

Shallow depth of field may isolate:

- knife
- onion
- fingertips

from the busy kitchen behind them.

## Sound

Kitchen ambience continues underneath.

Foreground:

    CHOP
    CHOP

Then the final chop begins.

Consider allowing the surrounding kitchen sound to become slightly quieter
in the mix as the danger becomes apparent.

Not complete silence.

Just enough reduction that the knife sound suddenly feels prominent.

## Music

No obvious suspense sting.

If music is present, it may subtly hold or thin out.

The visual and repetitive chopping sound should create the tension.

## Generation Notes

Hands are the primary generation risk.

Watch carefully for:

- extra fingers
- missing fingers
- fingers changing length
- fingers merging with onion
- knife changing shape
- knife bending
- knife passing through onion unnaturally
- knife contacting the finger
- unexpected injury
- excessive chopping speed
- cutting board changing
- onion changing shape dramatically
- photorealistic style drift

The shot does NOT need complicated motion.

A successful generation can consist almost entirely of:

    knife rises
        +
    fingers shift slightly
        +
    knife begins downward motion

If the generated hand anatomy becomes unstable, shorten the usable portion
of the clip in Resolve.

This is an editing shot more than a performance shot.

It may ultimately last only 1.5–2 seconds in the finished film.

The audience only needs enough time to recognize:

    KNIFE
