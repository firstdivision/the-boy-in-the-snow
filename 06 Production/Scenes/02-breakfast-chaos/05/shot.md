# Shot 05 — Madrigal Sees the Danger

## Status

Starting Frame Needed

## Estimated Duration

~2 seconds

## Purpose

Show the instant Madrigal realizes why she was sent to the restaurant.

She sees the chef's fingertip directly in the path of the descending knife.

There is almost no time to react.

This is the moment Madrigal switches from slightly frazzled observer to
focused guardian.

## Story Beat

"...had Madrigal not been there to move the knife blade out of the way
at just the right moment."

## Composition

Tight close-up or tight medium close-up of Madrigal.

Her face is the primary focus.

Frame approximately from upper chest upward while allowing part of her
folded wings to remain visible behind her.

Madrigal is looking slightly downward and off-camera toward the chef's
cutting board.

The busy kitchen remains visible only as a soft, warm blur behind her.

## Character

Maintain Madrigal's established appearance:

- youthful stylized animated facial design
- dark hair loosely gathered into a messy bun
- dark blue-gray winter coat
- thick scarf
- large white feathered wings
- normal young-adult proportions

Her wings remain folded.

Do not make her photorealistic.

## Starting Expression

At the beginning of the shot, Madrigal is watching the chef with focused
attention.

She has not yet fully understood the danger.

Her expression is neutral but alert.

## Action

Madrigal's eyes track something below and off-camera.

Then she realizes what is about to happen.

Her eyes widen slightly.

Her eyebrows lift.

She takes one small, involuntary gasp.

Her lips part very slightly.

There is a tiny intake of breath.

Then almost immediately her expression changes.

Surprise disappears.

Her eyes lock onto the danger.

Her face becomes focused and decisive.

Her body begins the very first movement forward.

CUT.

The entire emotional progression happens quickly:

    watching
       ↓
    realization
       ↓
    tiny gasp
       ↓
    immediate focus
       ↓
    move

## Performance

The reaction must be subtle.

This is NOT:

- terror
- screaming
- exaggerated shock
- a huge gasp
- cartoon panic

Madrigal is surprised because she suddenly understands the danger.

But she is also trained for this.

The gasp lasts only an instant.

Her professional instinct takes over immediately.

The audience should see:

    Oh!

followed almost instantly by:

    MOVE.

## Wings

Her wings remain folded throughout most of the shot.

They may tense very slightly at the moment of realization.

A tiny feather movement is acceptable.

Do not open or spread the wings.

## Camera

Tight cinematic close-up.

Camera remains nearly stationary.

A very subtle push toward Madrigal's face is acceptable.

No orbit.

No camera shake.

No dramatic zoom.

No dramatic sting.

A tiny musical tension cue may begin as she realizes the danger, but the

    she sees it

The next shot completes the movement.

## Generation Notes

The facial performance is the entire purpose of this shot.

Watch specifically for:

- exaggerated surprise
- mouth opening too far
- cartoonishly large eyes
- talking or lip-sync-like movement
- head jerking backward
- prolonged gasp
- character becoming photorealistic
- character becoming younger or childlike
- hairstyle changing
- wings opening
- extra feathers or wings
- background characters becoming distracting

The ideal performance is SMALL.

The strongest take may consist of little more than:

    eyes widen
    tiny breath
    expression firms
    body starts forward

Do not add additional actions.

## Production Notes

This shot will probably be extremely short in the final edit.

Two seconds may even be generous.

Its job is not to stop the action.

It gives us one instant inside Madrigal's experience before she intervenes.

The gasp is especially useful because it makes the reaction feel instinctive
rather than calculated.

She doesn't have time to think.

She sees the danger and moves.
