# Shot 06 — The Intervention

## Status

Starting Frame Needed

## Estimated Duration

~2–3 seconds

## Purpose

Show Madrigal performing the tiny intervention that prevents the chef from
cutting his fingertip.

Her action is almost insignificant:

She moves the knife sideways by just enough.

That tiny change prevents the injury.

## Story Beat

"...had Madrigal not been there to move the knife blade out of the way
at just the right moment."

## Composition

Tight medium close-up of the chef's preparation area.

The chef's hands, knife, onion, and cutting board are clearly visible.

Madrigal enters the composition from one side.

Only as much of Madrigal as necessary needs to be visible:

- part of her face or upper body
- one arm
- one hand

Her hand reaches toward the SIDE of the knife.

The chef remains unaware of her presence.

## Action

The knife has begun its dangerous downward movement.

Madrigal reacts immediately.

She reaches in quickly and gives the SIDE of the knife a small lateral nudge.

She does NOT grab the blade.

She does NOT catch the knife.

She does NOT touch the chef.

The intervention changes the knife's trajectory by only a few inches.

Instead of descending toward the chef's fingertip, the knife moves safely
to the side.

The chef's hand remains unharmed.

The unexpected sideways movement causes the chef to lose his grip on the
knife.

CUT as the knife begins coming loose from his hand.

Shot 07 completes that action.

## Madrigal Performance

Madrigal is completely focused.

There is no hesitation.

Her movement should feel:

- quick
- precise
- practiced
- instinctive

She has done this kind of thing many times before.

There is nothing magical-looking about the intervention.

She simply changes one tiny physical detail at exactly the right moment.

## Chef Performance

The chef is still distracted.

He does not see Madrigal.

He does not intentionally release the knife.

His expression is not important in this shot.

His hand simply reacts naturally when the knife unexpectedly changes
direction.

## Camera

Tight cinematic shot.

Mostly locked camera.

No orbit.

No dramatic camera movement.

No slow motion generated by the video model.

If we want to manipulate timing, do it later in Resolve.

## Video Model

## Video Prompt

Tight cinematic animated shot of the dangerous kitchen moment shown in the
reference image.

Preserve the young winged woman's appearance, the cook's hands, chef's knife,
onion, cutting board, clothing, warm kitchen lighting, colors, composition,
and painterly animated-film aesthetic shown in the reference image.

The chef's knife has begun a natural downward chopping movement while one
fingertip is accidentally positioned too close to its path.

The young winged woman reacts immediately.

With one quick, precise movement, she reaches toward the knife and gently
nudges the SIDE of the blade sideways with her fingertips.

She does not grab the knife and does not touch the sharp cutting edge.

Her movement changes the knife's trajectory only slightly, redirecting it
safely away from the chef's fingertip.

The chef's finger remains completely unharmed.

Because of the unexpected sideways movement, the knife begins slipping from
the chef's grip.

End the shot just as the knife starts coming loose from his hand.

The intervention is small, precise, and physically believable rather than
dramatic or magical.

Keep all hand anatomy stable and natural. Keep the knife rigid and consistent
in shape and size.

No injury. No blood. No wound. No contact between blade and finger. No knife
throwing. No exaggerated action. No dramatic magic effect. No glowing light.
No wing movement.

Preserve the stylized animated appearance of the characters. Do not make
them photorealistic or live-action.

Quick, restrained, precisely timed intervention.

Approximately 2 seconds.

## Generation Notes

This is one of the highest-risk AI generation shots because it combines:

- two sets of hands
- a knife
- precise physical interaction

Keep the generated action VERY simple.

Watch specifically for:

- extra fingers
- fused hands
- Madrigal grabbing the sharp edge
- knife passing through a hand
- knife bending
- blade touching the chef's finger
- accidental injury
- knife disappearing
- Madrigal's arm changing length
- chef's hand changing position impossibly
- dramatic knife flight
- magical effects
- photorealistic style drift

If Kling struggles with the physical interaction, do NOT keep adding prompt
instructions.

Instead, simplify the shot.

Fallback approach:

Show Madrigal's fingertip already extremely close to the flat side of the
knife.

Generate only:

    tiny sideways tap
          ↓
    knife changes direction

We can create the apparent speed of the rescue through editing.

## Production Notes

The thematic point of the entire sequence is contained in this action.

Madrigal does not perform a spectacular rescue.

She changes something by an inch.

Nobody notices.

But that inch matters.

The audience knows that without it, the chef's day — and perhaps much more —
would have been very different.
