# Shot 08 — The Smile Disappears

## Status

Starting Frame Needed

## Estimated Duration

~6 seconds

## Purpose

Show the emotional cost of Madrigal's invisible work.

She has just prevented a painful injury and, for one brief moment, feels
proud of herself.

The chef responds not with gratitude, but with anger at the knife that
apparently slipped from his hand.

He has no idea Madrigal saved him.

Her little moment of triumph disappears.

## Story Beat

"Her intervention caused the knife to come out of the chef's hand who then
cursed loudly, oblivious to the wound he had narrowly escaped.

What had been a triumphant smile on Madrigal's face vanished at his outburst,
replaced by a look of defeat."

## Composition

Medium close-up of Madrigal beside the preparation station.

Madrigal is the clear focus.

The chef should remain visible behind or beside her, but slightly softer in
the composition.

The fallen knife and partially chopped onion may remain visible near the
bottom of the frame if composition permits.

Madrigal's wings remain folded behind her.

## Starting Image

`starting-image.png`

## Starting Image Requirements

Begin immediately after Shot 07.

Madrigal is looking toward the chef's unharmed hand and the fallen knife.

A small, relieved, triumphant smile has just appeared.

The chef is looking down toward the knife with the bewildered expression
established at the end of Shot 07.

Maintain:

- Madrigal's established character design
- chef's established character design
- tall white chef's hat
- full dark beard and mustache
- stocky build
- white chef coat
- black pinstriped apron
- partially chopped onion
- warm Copper Kettle kitchen
- painterly animated-film aesthetic

## Action

Madrigal looks at the chef's unharmed hand.

Then at the fallen knife.

She smiles.

It is small, warm, and private.

For a moment she is genuinely pleased with herself.

Behind her, the chef stares at the knife in bewilderment.

His confusion turns into irritation.

He curses loudly.

Madrigal's smile immediately falters.

Her eyes shift toward him.

The smile disappears.

Her shoulders lower slightly.

Her eyes fall.

The excitement and pride leave her expression and are replaced by quiet
disappointment.

She does not become melodramatically sad.

She simply looks tired.

This has happened before.

## Performance

The emotional progression should be clearly readable but restrained:

    relief
       ↓
    tiny triumphant smile
       ↓
    chef curses
       ↓
    smile falters
       ↓
    disappointment
       ↓
    tired resignation

The most important detail is the transition.

Do not jump immediately from happy to miserable.

Let us SEE the smile disappear.

## Chef Performance

The chef remains unaware of Madrigal.

He looks at the fallen knife.

His expression begins with:

    What the hell?

Then becomes irritation.

He gestures toward the knife or preparation counter and curses.

He is angry at the situation, NOT intentionally angry at Madrigal.

He never looks directly at her.

## Camera

Medium close-up.

Mostly stationary.

A very slow, subtle push toward Madrigal is acceptable as her smile fades.

Do not pan toward the chef when he curses.

Stay with Madrigal.

His reaction is important because of what it does to HER.

## Video Model

Kling 3.0 Pro

## Video Prompt

Cinematic medium close-up continuing directly from the reference image inside the warm animated restaurant kitchen.

Preserve both characters, clothing, proportions, kitchen, lighting, composition, and painterly animated-film style.

IMPORTANT: The chef cannot see the young winged woman and is completely unaware she is beside him. He must NEVER look toward her, turn toward her, speak toward her, gesture toward her, or acknowledge her.

At first, the young woman looks at the chef's unharmed hand and the fallen knife. Realizing her intervention succeeded, a small genuine smile of relief and quiet pride appears on her face. Let this moment breathe briefly.

The chef keeps his eyes DOWN on the fallen knife. He stares at it with a puzzled, bewildered expression, trying to understand why it slipped from his hand.

His attention remains entirely on the knife and cutting board.

His bewilderment gradually becomes irritation. Still looking down, he frowns and makes a small frustrated gesture toward the knife. He mutters angrily about what happened without looking away from his work surface.

Hearing his reaction, the young woman's smile falters. She looks toward him briefly, but he never looks back.

Her smile slowly disappears. Her shoulders lower slightly and her gaze falls. Her expression becomes quietly disappointed and tired. She does not cry or dramatically frown.

Her white feathered wings remain folded behind her.

Background cooks continue normal kitchen activity and do not acknowledge her.

Camera remains nearly stationary with a very slow cinematic push toward the young woman's face.

No eye contact between them. The chef never acknowledges her. His attention remains on the fallen knife. No one reacts to her presence. No wing spreading. No exaggerated gestures.

Preserve the stylized painterly animated appearance. Do not become photorealistic.

Duration: 6–8 seconds.

## Sound

Continue the Copper Kettle ambience:

- sizzling pans
- dishes
- kitchen voices
- ventilation
- utensils
- footsteps

The knife clatter from Shot 07 has just occurred.

Give the chef a short beat of confused silence.

Then his curse cuts through the kitchen noise.

Example:

    CHEF
    Ah, goddammit!

or simply:

    CHEF
    Dammit!

His voice should sound irritated rather than terrified.

Madrigal says nothing.

A very subtle feather or clothing rustle may accompany her shoulders
dropping.

## Music

Music should remain restrained.

A tiny warm musical phrase may accompany Madrigal's initial smile.

When the chef curses, allow that phrase to stop or resolve downward.

Avoid an obvious "sad music" cue.

The disappointment should come primarily from Madrigal's face.

## Continuity

### From Shot 07

Shot 07 ends with:

- knife safely on counter
- chef unharmed

- chef bewildered
- Madrigal relieved

Shot 08 begins essentially at the same moment.

### Into Shot 09

Madrigal ends Shot 08 looking defeated.

Shot 09 continues the emotional response:

She wraps her wings around herself for comfort.

This should feel less like a supernatural action and more like someone
pulling a blanket around their shoulders after a bad day.

## Transition

Shot 07 may end with the chef beginning his vocal reaction.

Alternatively, Shot 08 can begin with a brief quiet beat before he curses.

Do not rush the smile.

The audience needs to see Madrigal feel good before that feeling is taken
away.

## Generation Notes

This is primarily a facial-performance shot.

Prioritize Madrigal over complicated background action.

Watch for:

- exaggerated smiling
- exaggerated sadness
- crying
- cartoon shock
- chef looking at Madrigal
- chef appearing to yell directly at Madrigal
- chef changing appearance
- beard disappearing
- chef's hat changing
- Madrigal becoming photorealistic
- Madrigal becoming childlike
- wings opening unexpectedly
- excessive body movement

If the model struggles with both performances, prioritize Madrigal.

The chef's anger can be strengthened later with off-screen dialogue and
sound design in Resolve.

## Production Notes

This is one of the first shots that explains who Madrigal is emotionally.

Saving the chef is not difficult for her.

Being unable to share the moment with him is.

She cannot say:

    "I just saved your finger."

He cannot say:

    "Thank you."

The audience is the only witness to what she did.

That is why we should let the shot breathe.

The chef's curse is almost incidental.

The story is in Madrigal's face.
